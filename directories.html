Most volumes before 1926 — the 1914 directory is the main exception — print only a name and an occupation, with no street address at all. That is how the books were published, not a gap in the transcription. Limiting to entries with addresses will therefore return little or nothing from those years; use Show all to search them by name or occupation, and see the scans for the original pages.

By street and number

Walk down a street

The main search answers “where did this person live?”. This answers the other question: who lived along this street? Pick a year, name a street, and the listings come back in house-number order — the way the printed criss-cross sections worked.

The original pages

Browse the scanned directories

Every volume behind this search is also available as a complete scan — 65 directories, 1870 to 1968. They are free to read online and free to download, hosted by the Internet Archive.

The scans show the printed page exactly as it was, advertisements and all, which is often worth seeing in its own right — and it is where to look when a transcribed entry seems wrong or a line is hard to make sense of.

Up to 1949 these are the Mountain View pages lifted out of the San Jose and Santa Clara County directory — Mountain View was not given a directory of its own until 1950, which is why the earlier page numbers run into the hundreds.

About these directories

City directories were published most years between 1870 and 1968, listing residents, their occupations and where they lived, along with local businesses. They are one of the best tools available for tracing a family or a building through time.

Entries here follow the original sources and the text recognition used to digitise them. Spellings vary, abbreviations are common, and some years are missing entirely — the gaps are in the historical record, not in the search.

Found something?

If a listing helps you piece together a family or a property, we would like to hear about it — those stories are exactly what the newsletter is for.

And if you spot an error in a transcription, tell us and we will look at the original.
