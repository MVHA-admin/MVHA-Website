Historical Landmarks

As one of the oldest communities in Silicon Valley, Mountain View is home to historic buildings stretching back to the mid-1800s.

Downtown Mountain View’s main street, Castro Street, is lined by an eclectic mix of structures from nearly every era of the city’s development, from the 1870s to today. The residential neighbourhoods flanking either side of Castro Street were mostly developed between the late 1800s and World War II. Small, lovingly maintained Craftsman and Spanish Revival bungalows, along with a scattering of older Victorian homes, have helped make these formerly working- and middle-class neighbourhoods some of the most sought-after real estate in the Bay Area.

The areas of Mountain View north of Central Expressway and south of El Camino Real were primarily agricultural until the 1950s. But if you are willing to search them out, you will find unique historic gems from earlier eras — usually preserved farmhouses or country residences now surrounded by modern development.

Four landmarks worth knowing

Built 1867 · National Register 1978

The Rengstorff House

The Rengstorff House is the oldest structure still standing in Mountain View. It was built in 1867 by Henry Rengstorff, a German immigrant who came to California during the Gold Rush but made his fortune farming in the Santa Clara Valley and operating a shipping port on Mountain View’s bay front known as Rengstorff Landing.

The house was built near the landing on Stierlin Road, now North Shoreline Boulevard. It was purchased by the City of Mountain View in 1979 and relocated to Shoreline Park, where it was eventually restored and opened to the public in 1991.

Built 1934 · Restored 2001

The Adobe Building

The Great Depression-era Adobe Building was Mountain View’s first community centre. In 1934, government work-relief labourers used adobe bricks made on site to construct the building’s walls, giving the building its name.

During World War II it served as a serviceman’s club and hospitality house for veterans. In the 1940s and 1950s it hosted weekly dances for local youth and was known as the “Eagle Shack”, after the mascot of Mountain View High School on Castro Street. Over the decades it has hosted countless club meetings, classes, weddings, dances and parties.

In the 1990s the MVHA led efforts to save the Adobe Building from demolition. It was seismically retrofitted and restored in 2001 and is now on the National Register of Historic Places. The MVHA hosts most of its quarterly membership meetings here.

The Save the Adobe campaign

Commissioned 1933

Moffett Field

Located just outside Mountain View’s city limits but within its county-designated sphere of influence, Moffett Field is one of the most historic sites in Santa Clara County. In 1844 the land was granted by the Mexican government as Rancho Posolmi to Lope Yñigo, a native Ohlone man — one of the few instances of Mexican-era ranchos granted to a California native.

Nearly a century later, local residents throughout the Bay Area banded together to buy 1,000 acres of the former rancho so they could sell it to the Navy for one dollar, establishing a naval air base that would serve as the Navy’s West Coast centre for its lighter-than-air programme. In 1939, NACA — now NASA Ames Research Center — was established on Moffett Field. Today the complex is home to an impressive assemblage of landmarks and districts on the National Register of Historic Places.

Moffett Field Museum

Built 1931

Hangar One

Hangar One is one of the world’s largest freestanding structures, covering eight acres at Moffett Field. Built to house the helium-filled rigid airship USS Macon, the massive hangar has long been one of the most recognisable landmarks of Silicon Valley.
