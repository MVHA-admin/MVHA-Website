Our History

Founded in 1954, the Mountain View Historical Association is dedicated to preserving and sharing the rich history of Mountain View, California. Here is how we got started.

On 9 September 1954 a small group of long-time Mountain View residents gathered to discuss the future of their city’s past. It was a time of unprecedented growth. New development was changing the face of the city, and the sons and daughters of its early pioneers realised that much of their hometown’s history risked being lost and forgotten.

The group decided to form a historical society to help preserve Mountain View’s history for future generations. They incorporated in 1958 as the Mountain View Pioneer and Historical Association, known today as the Mountain View Historical Association.

Seventy years of preservation

For over seventy years, the Association has contributed in many ways toward preserving the heritage of the local area. Its historical publications, including Milestones: A History of Mountain View, are the premier sources of information about local people, places and events of the past. Its markers and plaques draw attention to local historic sites. The Association takes part in community events and many special occasions where information, exhibits and speakers are requested.

Of particular importance is the Association’s irreplaceable collection of archival material: scrapbooks, yearbooks, diaries, photographs, maps and other small artifacts of local significance. Before 1967, historical items were stored in private homes. As more space was needed, a permanent location on the second floor of the Mountain View Public Library was secured. Volunteers maintain, grow and share the History Center’s archives for the benefit of the community.

Saving the Adobe

In 1995, the Association’s “Save the Adobe” campaign halted the demolition of the circa 1934 Adobe Building on Moffett Boulevard. The building was fully restored by the City of Mountain View in 2001 and is now listed on the National Register of Historic Places. It is where the Association holds most of its quarterly meetings today.

The Association has long hoped to build a museum for the collection and display of larger historical artifacts, and continues to seek out new places to share Mountain View’s history. For now, large artifacts owned by the organisation have been loaned to other local historical museums.

Quarterly gatherings

The public is warmly welcomed to attend the Association’s quarterly events at the historic Adobe Building. Events typically feature a guest speaker and a presentation on a topic of local historic interest. The Association’s newsletter, The Mountain ReView, is issued before each session and previews the upcoming event, updates on Association activities, and articles of historical interest.

Mountain View has changed a great deal since the Historical Association was formed in 1954. But thanks to the foresight of the Association’s pioneer founders and the hard work of countless members, Mountain View’s rich history will not be forgotten.
